
## 1. A/B Testing

Think of a traditional hypothesis-testing problem. <br>
Suppose you want to know whether the average weight of cattle differs from 500 kg. <br>
You might test \(H_0: \mu = 500\) versus \(H_A: \mu \neq 500\) using a one-sample t-test on a sample of cattle weights. <br>

<br>

Now suppose you want to know, "does a new feed increase cattle weight gain compared with the current feed?" <br>
You could randomly assign cattle to either: **A:** current feed or **B:** new feed. <br>
Then compare the weight gain between the two groups. <br>

The main difference is that A/B testing refers to setting up a randomized experiment, while a hypothesis test is a statistical method used to analyze data. A/B testing often uses hypothesis tests to determine whether the difference between A and B is likely due to the treatment rather than random variation. <br>

A/B testing follows the same basic idea as randomized clinical trials and other controlled experiments. <br>

A/B testing became widely adopted in user-experience and product research, because digital products make it easy to randomly assign users to different versions of a product and measure their responses. <br>
